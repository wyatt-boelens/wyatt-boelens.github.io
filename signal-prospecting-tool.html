CASE STUDY — SIGNAL-BASED OUTBOUND

Signal Prospecting Tool

A signal-based outbound system run by Claude with Apollo and a Google Sheet. It finds the moment an account is most likely to say yes, proves it with a dated public source, routes it to the person who can act, and hands a rep a short list to approve before anything is sent.

A public, ready-to-use template, MIT licensed. This one is the real thing, not a reconstruction: the rules, the Sheet template and the prompts below are the actual files in the repository. Anything company-specific is left as a [bracketed placeholder]. Built on lessons from an internal dry run.

The problem

Outbound usually runs on fit alone: the right kind of company, contacted whenever the list gets to it. Timing is the missing half. A leader who just changed jobs, a company that just launched a program, a role that was just posted — each marks a moment when a message is far more likely to land.

But timing signals fail in familiar ways. A "signal" with no dated source is really a guess. Hits pile up on accounts a rep is already working. A confident-sounding message quietly leans on a claim nobody confirmed. And nothing in the process ever shows whether any of it beats plain outreach.

This tool turns each of those failure modes into a rule Claude checks on every run — timing on top of fit, never instead of it — and keeps a person in the loop for every send.

How a hit moves through the system
every hit travels the same path — failing any hard stop drops it, and nothing moves without a rep's Y
Where everything lives

No code to deploy. The "tool" is three parts that Claude reads and writes each run, each with a different owner. Facts that change as a company grows live in the Sheet, where one cell updates them. The rules change rarely, and only through the Admin.

Who writes what

Every Log row carries the rep's name in column W, and a rep only approves and acts on their own rows. Cooldowns and exclusions apply across all reps, and the first rep to log an account owns it — if another rep already has a row for it, the tool skips it and tells the Admin.

The architecture
01
Evidence, or it isn't a signal

Every hit needs the original source URL and its original publish date, inside that signal's freshness window. No URL or no date means no signal. Sources are public only: the tool never works around a login, paywall or blocked site, and it verifies each company by its domain.

02
Route to whoever owns the function the signal is about

The first question is who owns the function this signal concerns. If nobody fits, the tool falls back to the department head (the founder or CEO at under about 50 people), but only someone who actually owns that function, and the copy keeps the signal's angle. It also confirms the person still works there.

03
Copy may only use what's confirmed

An opener can draw on exactly four things: the logged fact, the signal's mapped angle, Claims rows marked Confirmed, and customers marked OK to cite. No invented quotes, numbers or customer names, and every number comes from the Claims tab at run time.

04
Timing decides when, never what's said

Some signals (funding, price increases, layoffs, departures, lawsuits, regulation, ad spend) are only useful for choosing the moment. They never appear in the message itself.

05
Signals shouldn't stack on active outreach

A hit is dropped if any rep touched the contact or account in the last 30 days, if the contact has been in five or more sequences, or if the company had distress news in the last 90 days. Accounts also get a 60-day cooldown across all reps, one contact per account per run, and a cap of 10 rows per rep per run. Losing hits to these rules is the point.

06
A human approves before anything moves

The tool never sends, never enrolls a contact in a sequence, and never edits one. It logs a row and shows the rep a short list. Only after the rep sets column O to Y does it create an Apollo task, and the rep sends from their own inbox. Paid Apollo calls need the rep's confirmation too, with a credit cap per run.

07
Facts live in the Sheet; rules live in the spec

Claims, customer proof, revenue thresholds and team members change as a company grows, so they sit in single Sheet cells that can be updated in one place. The rules change rarely and only through the Admin, so they sit in one file inside a Claude Project. The signal library has exactly one copy, in the Signals tab.

08
Every signal is a hypothesis until the numbers say otherwise

A new signal starts as a Hypothesis. It's marked Proven at 5% or more positive replies after 30+ sent (adjustable), and Cut or reworked if there are zero positive after 30. The whole program has a kill rule too: at 60 sent it needs 3+ positive; 1–2 extends the test to 120 (needing 6+); zero means stop and rework. Replies are judged from the actual reply text, since Apollo's replied count includes bounces and auto-replies and open rates are unreliable.

The ten hard stops

Every hit has to pass all ten. Fail one and the row is dropped.

H1
Evidence

Original source URL and publish date, inside the signal's freshness window. No URL or date, no signal.

H2
Exclusions

Drop current customers, open deals, do-not-contact, closed-won, unsubscribed and bounced. Matches on domain and name aliases, so rebrands and duplicates don't slip through. A current-customer signal becomes an account-management note, never outreach.

H3
Contact history

Skip if the contact ever said no, any rep touched them in the last 30 days, they've been in 5+ sequences, or the company had distress news in the last 90 days. A "not now" gets scheduled for their date instead.

H4
Volume

One contact per account per run, a 60-day account cooldown across all reps, and at most 10 rows per rep per run. Paid Apollo calls need the rep's confirmation and a credit cap.

H5
Human approval

Never send, enroll in a sequence, or edit a sequence. Tasks are created only after the rep marks Y.

H6
Truth

Copy uses only the logged fact, the signal's mapped angle, Confirmed claims and citable customers. No invented quotes, numbers or names.

H7
Routing

The contact's persona must be allowed for that signal, and they must still work there. If the seat is open, fall back to the department head who owns the function.

H8
Sources

Public sources only. Never work around a login, paywall or blocked site. Verify the company by its domain.

H9
Compliance

EU and UK contacts are flagged for the rep's review before they're logged. No legal commentary in copy.

H10
Measurement

Judge replies from the reply text. Apollo's replied count includes bounces and auto-replies, and opens are unreliable.

Running it

Setup takes about 30 minutes. After that, a run is two prompts: one to find and log hits, and one, about two weeks after sending, to read the results. Paste the first into a chat inside the Claude Project that holds the rules file, replacing the bracketed parts.

prompts/weekly-run.md
Run the signal tool weekly run for [your name]. Follow SPEC.md exactly. Read the Signals, Scoreboard, Claims, Peer Proof and Reps tabs of [your Sheet name or link] first. Log up to 10 verified hits to the Log tab with my name in column W, then show me the short list to approve. Ask before any paid Apollo search.

After the run, the rep sets Approved (O) to Y or N on their rows, tells Claude to create the Apollo tasks for the Y rows, sends, and fills Date sent (Q).

prompts/results-update.md
Update the signal Log results for my rows sent 14 or more days ago. Read the actual reply text in my Gmail and fill Reply type (R) and Meeting booked (S). Bounces and auto-replies count as "Bounce or auto-reply", not as replies.

Once a month the Admin reviews the Scoreboard: signals marked Proven stay Active, signals marked "Cut or rework" get rewritten or switched off, and new learnings go into the rules file. The advice for the first few weeks: run it by hand for 2 to 3 weeks before turning it into a saved skill or a scheduled task, because the first runs always change the rules.

What the first dry run taught
  • Job-change data has no date filter. Scan once per persona keyword instead of scanning every job change.
  • Broad web searches mostly return huge brands. Searching for "launched an affiliate program" or "announced a partnership" doesn't surface the right accounts; Google Alerts delivered to Gmail work better.
  • "Hiring for" filters over-match. Apollo's filter also matches partner-sales roles, so every posting gets confirmed on a job board.
  • The 30-day rule removes a lot of hits. That's the point: signals shouldn't stack on top of active outreach.
  • An open seat doesn't kill the signal. Route to the department head who owns that function, and keep the signal's angle.
Why this matters

Most outbound automation optimizes for volume. This one is built the other way around: it's allowed to drop hits, it can't send on its own, it can only say things that have been confirmed, and it carries its own test for whether it's working. The result is fewer, better-timed touches that a person approved, plus a Scoreboard that can tell you if the whole thing isn't beating plain outreach.

Where this stops

This is a v1.0 template built from the lessons of one internal dry run, so this page claims no results. The Scoreboard exists to find out whether the system beats your own baseline: your positive replies divided by contacts emailed, over roughly the last 90 days.

It also doesn't replace a person. It doesn't send, it doesn't decide whether an account is worth pursuing, and it doesn't give legal advice (EU and UK contacts are flagged for the rep's review rather than judged). It needs Claude with Apollo, Gmail, Google Sheets and Google Drive connected, an Apollo list of qualified accounts, and a company Google account for the Sheet.

Use it yourself
  1. Sheet: upload template/Signal_Log_Template.xlsx to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets (File → Save as Google Sheets).
  2. Fill the fact tabs: Claims, Peer Proof and Reps. Delete the example rows.
  3. Edit the Signals tab: set Active = Y for the 3 to 5 signals you'll start with, and rewrite each mapped angle for your product.
  4. Set your baseline on the Scoreboard: your current positive-reply rate per contact emailed.
  5. Apollo: create a contacts list for touched contacts.
  6. Rules: add the rules file to a Claude Project and fill in the bracketed placeholders.
  7. Optional: set up Google Alerts delivered to a Gmail label the tool reads.
