The Buying Committee Router

A rules-based AI agent that generates a working hypothesis of each contact's buying-committee role, matches them to the right proof point, and sequences multi-threaded accounts — the same logic whether you're selling into manufacturing, financial services, healthcare, construction, or software.

Genericized reconstruction for portfolio purposes. The role-classification, sequencing, and proof-point logic is real and mine; the seller, reference customers, and prospects below are fictional.

The problem

Personalizing outreach at scale usually forces a bad trade-off: spend real time per prospect figuring out who they are on the buying committee and what they'd actually care about, or send something generic and accept a weaker reply rate. That trade-off gets worse once a list spans multiple industries, multiple contacts at the same account, or both — a title that signals "champion" in one vertical can mean something completely different in another, and two contacts from the same company usually deserve a sequence, not two identical one-off emails.

The fix is turning the judgment call a good rep makes instinctively — who is this person on the deal, what do they actually own, what would make them reply, and who should get approached first — into an explicit rule set an agent can apply consistently, without overstating what it actually knows.

The architecture
01
Classify by buying-committee role — and treat it as a hypothesis, not a fact

Only true C-suite (CEO, COO, CFO, CRO, Founder, President, any "Chief ___ Officer") routes to Economic Buyer; VP/SVP/EVP routes to Executive Sponsor; Director/Head-of level in the affected function routes to Champion; IC-level in that function routes to End User; and IT/Security/Ops/Procurement routes to Technical Evaluator regardless of seniority. But every assignment ships with the discovery question that would actually confirm it. A title-based label is an assumption until a real conversation validates it — treating it as settled fact is the exact mistake MEDDIC discipline exists to catch.

02
Match proof points on segment overlap, not just industry label

Check for a same-vertical reference customer first. If none exists, fall back to the closest adjacent match based on comparable buying motion, company size, or operational profile — rather than forcing a same-label reference that doesn't actually resonate with this specific buyer.

03
Surface weak fits, and change what the system spends effort on

A title with no clear path to a purchase decision still appears in the output with a visible flag — but it also gets a short, low-touch line instead of a fully tailored pain angle and opener. A flag that doesn't change downstream behavior is just decoration; this one actually reallocates effort toward the contacts worth the effort.

04
Sequence multi-threaded accounts instead of scoring contacts in isolation

When two or more contacts from the same company appear in a list, the system doesn't just classify them independently — it recommends who to approach first and why, based on which role is best positioned to gather intel versus which role needs that intel in hand before being approached.

05
Output in the format the next step actually uses

Structured as a table that drops straight into a CRM or sequencing tool — no reformatting step between the system's output and a rep's next action. Role, proof point, pain angle, and opener as separate columns, not a paragraph to be parsed by hand.

Why this matters

This replaces a judgment call a strong rep makes prospect-by-prospect with a rule set that runs the same way at 5 prospects or 500, in one vertical or five — and gets more useful, not less, as more contacts from the same account show up in the list. The quality of the first pass doesn't degrade as volume or account depth goes up, which is normally exactly where manual personalization breaks down.

Where this stops

This is a top-of-funnel routing and prioritization tool, not a full qualification engine. It generates a role hypothesis and a starting sequence — it doesn't replace discovery. MEDDIC's Decision Criteria and Decision Process, for instance, can only be confirmed through actual conversations; no title-based system should claim to infer them, and this one doesn't try.

A production version would go two directions from here: research-augmented personalization that pulls real per-contact signals instead of relying on role-level templates, and outcome-weighted matching that improves its proof-point and reframe choices based on which ones actually generate replies and meetings over time — the system gets sharper with use instead of staying static.
